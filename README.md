# Task 1 — AI-Assisted Mentor–Parent Communication

## 🎯 Problem Overview

Academic mentors may need to communicate with parents regularly regarding student progress, attendance, concerns, and follow-up actions. Handling these interactions manually can require considerable time and consistent record-keeping.

This task explores a proposed AI-assisted approach to make the communication process more structured and efficient while retaining human control over important decisions.

## 💡 Proposed Solution

The proposal adapts ideas from a voice-based receptionist workflow to an academic communication use case.

The intended workflow combines structured information collection, AI-assisted processing, record management, and human review. The aim is to reduce repetitive administrative effort without removing the mentor's responsibility for sensitive or consequential communication.

## 🔑 Key Areas

- **Workflow analysis:** Understand the existing process and identify opportunities for improvement.
- **AI-assisted processing:** Explore how information can be organized, summarized, and prepared for review.
- **Human oversight:** Require human approval for consequential communications and actions.
- **Data privacy:** Minimize unnecessary exposure of student and parent information.
- **Error handling:** Account for incomplete information, ambiguous inputs, and system failures.
- **Impact analysis:** Estimate potential time savings and operational benefits using clearly identified assumptions.
- **Implementation planning:** Outline a staged approach for developing and evaluating the proposed workflow.

## 🔗 Related Project — Receptionist.AI

**Repository:** [github.com/AnnadataHemanth/receptionist.ai](https://github.com/AnnadataHemanth/receptionist.ai)

Receptionist.AI is a voice-based receptionist project developed using Python, FastAPI, Twilio, and database-backed appointment workflows.

The existing project provides the technical foundation for exploring a related communication use case. The mentor–parent workflow is a proposed adaptation, not a claim that the existing receptionist already implements a complete academic communication platform.

# Task 2 — Curriculum Audit & 2030 Readiness

## 🎯 Objective

Evaluate curriculum priorities for a B.Tech in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science and propose changes that could strengthen preparation for future AI and software engineering roles.

The goal is not simply to add more AI subjects. It is to connect strong mathematical and computing foundations with the practical ability to build, test, deploy, evaluate, and responsibly maintain AI-enabled systems.

## 🧭 Curriculum Review Framework

The recommendations use four decision categories:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **KEEP** | Retain important foundational subjects and learning outcomes. |
| **UPDATE** | Refresh existing content, practical work, or assessment methods. |
| **REDUCE** | Consider reducing duplicated or low-value material after reviewing the detailed syllabus. |
| **ADD** | Introduce missing practical capabilities or integrated learning opportunities where appropriate. |

These categories help distinguish essential foundations from areas where teaching methods, assessments, or course integration could be improved.

## 🔑 Key Areas

### 1. Curriculum and Industry Alignment

Examine the relationship between academic learning and the skills commonly associated with AI and software engineering roles, including programming, algorithms, databases, machine learning, data processing, testing, deployment, and responsible AI.

### 2. Practical AI Engineering

Recommend more opportunities for students to demonstrate their skills through reproducible experiments, software testing, API development, data validation, model evaluation, deployment workflows, and technical documentation.

### 3. AI-Assisted Learning

Explore how AI tools can support learning and development while requiring independent verification. Illustrative exercises cover areas such as algorithms, databases, machine learning, natural language processing, and GenAI workflow design.

### 4. Credit-Impact Analysis

Use a hypothetical planning scenario to examine the proportion of curriculum content that could be affected by proposed changes. The calculation is illustrative and depends on the assumed credit baseline and the definition of affected credits.

### 5. International Student Exchange

Propose possible exchange pathways, including virtual collaboration, joint projects, and potential research or semester-exchange opportunities. These are recommendations rather than confirmed institutional partnerships.

### 6. Integrated AI Systems Capstone

Use the mentor–parent communication concept to demonstrate how multiple areas of study can come together in an AI-enabled system, including requirements, data handling, APIs, AI-assisted processing, human approval, security, testing, and evaluation.

## 🧪 Methodology and Evidence

The assessment uses structured problem analysis, proposed system workflows, curriculum decision frameworks, illustrative calculations, and hypothetical scenarios to develop its recommendations.

To keep the conclusions transparent:

- Hypothetical assumptions are distinguished from verified facts.
- Illustrative calculations are not presented as measured real-world outcomes.
- Simulated AI examples are not represented as live experiments.
- Proposed curriculum changes are not represented as officially approved university decisions.
- The existing Receptionist.AI project is distinguished from the proposed academic communication adaptation.

This approach allows the assessment to explore possible solutions without overstating the evidence available.
